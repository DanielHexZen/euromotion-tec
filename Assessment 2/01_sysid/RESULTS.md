Generated by `sysid_main.m` (all numbers below are printed by the script; sampling time T = 0.02 s everywhere).

## 1. Data, preprocessing, protocol

- Source: `data/openloop_test01_synced.mat` (100 Hz grid), decimated by 2 (every 2nd sample) -> T = 0.02 s, N = 5261 samples (105.2 s).
- NaN handling (`common/load_decimated.m`): commands (`cmd_speed`, `servo_steering`) zero-order hold; sensors linear interpolation of the short gaps (<= 70 ms); 6 interpolated samples in total, **0 inside the moving period used for identification/validation** (all gaps are at the start/end of the log).
- Signals: longitudinal `u = cmd_speed`, `y = vesc_speed` [m/s]; lateral `y = r = imu_wz - gyro bias` [rad/s], `u = u_lat` (Section 7).
- Gyro bias from the post-run standstill (t > 90.1 s, n = 757): **-4.054e-04 rad/s**, std 8.244e-04 rad/s (pre-run window, noisier, presumably because the car was still being handled: mean 8.397e-04, std 8.985e-03 rad/s).
- Moving period: t = 7.66 ... 86.96 s (3966 samples) = from the first sample with wheel speed >= 1.0 m/s until 0.1 s before the stop command. It is cut into 6 contiguous blocks of 13.2 s. **Blocks 1,3,5 identification, blocks 2,4,6 validation** (alternating segments, SPEC option). Every block is an independent experiment: LS regressors never cross a block border; free-run simulations restart at each block start from the measured initial samples (n0 = max(na, nk+nb-1) samples) and are then driven by u only (no output feedback).
- Excluded on purpose: the start-up from rest (the wheel speed stays at zero although the command is already ~0.7 m/s: static friction / dead zone) and the braking to standstill (VESC brake ramp, about -5 m/s^2, slew-rate limited). Both are strongly non-linear and not part of the linear operating regime; their effect on the fit is reported honestly in Section 6 (protocol P5).
- Metrics: NRMSE fit = 100(1 - ||y - yhat|| / ||y - mean(y)||) [%]; R^2 = 1 - SS_res/SS_tot; pooled over all blocks of a set. One-step-ahead (OSA, uses measured past outputs) and free-run (simulation) are both reported.

## 2. Least-squares formulation

ARX: `y(k) = -a1 y(k-1) - ... - a_na y(k-na) + b1 u(k-nk) + ... + b_nb u(k-nk-nb+1) + e(k)`.

Regression row `phi(k) = [-y(k-1) ... -y(k-na)  u(k-nk) ... u(k-nk-nb+1)]`, stacked `Phi` (N x (na+nb)) and `Y = [y(k)]`, `Y = Phi*theta + e`, `theta = [a1..a_na b1..b_nb]^T`.
Solved with `theta = Phi\Y` (QR); identical to the normal equations `(Phi^T Phi) theta = Phi^T Y` (checked: difference 4.2e-13 / 9.4e-13 for lon / lat; cond(Phi^T Phi) = 4.84e+03 / 3.11e+03). Implementation: `common/arx_ls.m`.

## 5. Continuous-time interpretation

Poles mapped with s = ln(z)/T (exact for ZOH sampling); time constant tau = -T/ln(p).

- **Longitudinal**: pole z = 0.911883 -> tau_v = **0.217 s** (s = -4.612 1/s), pure delay n_k T = 0.020 s (one sampling period: command -> wheel speed), DC gain 1.0010 (unity steady-state tracking). Equivalent first-order lag with delay: G_v(s) ~ 1.001 e^{-0.020 s}/(0.217 s + 1). SPEC pre-study: tau_v ~ 0.28 s (plausibly a lag + delay lumped together: tau + delay = 0.237 s here).
- **Lateral**: complex pole pair z = 0.6561 +- 0.2255i -> s = -18.28 +- 16.55i 1/s, natural frequency omega_n = **24.7 rad/s** (3.9 Hz), damping zeta = **0.74**, pure delay n_k T = 0.060 s, DC gain k_delta = **1.7281** (yaw-rate per unit of the corrected kinematic yaw-rate demand u_lat). The dynamic part is the steering servo + tyre/yaw response (servo lag ~ 0.1 s in the SPEC pre-study = 60 ms delay + the second-order lag).
- Gyro bias -4.05e-04 rad/s is negligible against the yaw-rate signal (std 0.96 rad/s in the moving period) but is subtracted.

## 7. Lateral model: non-linear scheduling and what was tried

The yaw-rate response is linear in the demand `u_lat` but the gain depends on speed and on the steering amplitude (understeer, servo/tyre saturation). Hammerstein/LPV-style static front end (`common/lat_input.m`), followed by a **linear** ARX model:

```
u_lat(k) = v(k) * tan(delta_cmd(k)) * (1 - cs*delta_cmd(k)^2) / ( L * (1 + Kus*v(k)^2) ),   L = 0.324 m
```

`v` = measured wheel speed, `delta_cmd` = `servo_steering` [rad, + left]. Kus = 0.055 s^2/m^2 (characteristic speed 1/sqrt(Kus) = 4.3 m/s) and cs = 1.50 1/rad^2 are estimated by a 2-D grid search (Kus 0..0.12, cs 0..3) maximising the **identification** free-run fit of the lateral ARX (validation untouched). The map is monotone in delta for |delta| <= 0.34 rad and all v in 0..3 m/s (checked numerically); `common/lat_inverse.m` returns the steering command that realises a desired u_lat at the current speed (bisection, saturated at +-delta_max).

| lateral input | (na,nb,nk) | ident. free-run % | valid. free-run % |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline v tan(delta)/L (Kus=0, cs=0) | (2,1,2) | 89.53 | 85.57 |
| + speed-dependent gain only (Kus=0.050, cs=0) | (2,1,3) | 90.95 | 90.37 |
| **+ steering saturation (Kus=0.055, cs=1.50) = final** | (2,1,3) | 91.94 | 91.66 |

Interpretation: the pure kinematic input explains the yaw rate only to ~86 % (validation) because the measured effective gain is ~1.4 (vs 1.0) and drops with speed; the static correction raises the fit above 90 % on identification and validation. The DC gain of G_r is k_delta = 1.7281 on the corrected input, i.e. an effective steering gain on v tan(delta)/L (small delta) of 1.638 / 1.416 / 1.156 at v = 1 / 2 / 3 m/s (SPEC pre-study: ~1.39 at ~2 m/s - consistent). Other attempts: higher orders and other delays (no gain, Section 3); a pure speed-dependent gain without steering saturation (row 2) recovers only part of the improvement.

How downstream packages use it: yaw-rate loop plant = `G_r(z)` from `u_lat` to `r`; the controller output `u_lat` is converted to the steering command with `delta_cmd = lat_inverse(u_lat, v, par)` (or approximately `delta = atan(L*u_lat/(k*v))` with k = k_eff(v)); the simulation plant integrates `psi_dot = r`, `x_dot = v cos psi`, `y_dot = v sin psi`. At v < 1 m/s the model is outside the identified range.

## 9. Design decisions (short rationale)

1. **T = 0.02 s, decimation by 2** - native VESC/IMU rate (SPEC).
2. **ARX + explicit LS** - the model is linear in the parameters, so `theta = Phi\Y` is the global optimum of the one-step prediction error; no iterative search, no local minima; closed form also gives std errors. ARX has no noise model, so the one-step fit is optimistic - the free-run fit is the honest figure (Section 6).
3. **Order/delay by structured search with a parsimony rule** - selection on identification free-run only; the first-order lon model is as good as any 2nd/3rd-order one (differences < 0.15 points), hence 1 state.
4. **nk >= 1** - strictly proper models for control design (sample-and-hold implementation delay). nk = 0 would add ~1.2 points (lon) but implies an algebraic loop in closed loop.
5. **Moving-regime identification** - start-up dead zone and slew-limited braking are non-linear and excluded; they are reported in P5.
6. **Alternating blocks for validation** - identification and validation sets both cover the full speed range; role swap (P2) and chronological hold-out (P3) are reported as well.
7. **Lateral Hammerstein/LPV input** - removes the speed dependence of the gain, keeps the dynamic part LTI (needed for pole-placement PID) and is exactly invertible for the steering command.
8. **Canonical forms written explicitly** (`common/tf2canon.m`), validated against `filter` (and the Control System Toolbox when present) to ~1e-15; same T = 0.02 s for tf and ss.
